# Exemplar Exam Essay 3: Social Media vs. Social Networking

**Question**: *Critically distinguish between "Social Media" and "Social Networking". Identify and discuss the five major structural differences between these platforms, and examine how organizations utilize each channel to communicate Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR). [20 Marks]*

---

## Model Answer (Full 20/20 Mark Rubric)

### 1. Introduction and Definitive Boundaries
In popular discourse, the terms "Social Media" and "Social Networking" are frequently conflated as interchangeable synonyms. In contemporary Information Science and political economy, however, they represent fundamentally distinct communication paradigms. Social Media is essentially an asymmetric content publishing and broadcast ecosystem designed for content consumption and mass dissemination. Conversely, Social Networking is an interactive, symmetric relationship-building infrastructure designed for the formation, maintenance, and mobilization of interpersonal social capital.

### 2. The Five Major Structural Differences

| Dimension | Social Media | Social Networking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Primary Purpose** | **Broadcast & Consumption**: Dissemination of rich media assets (videos, articles, memes) to maximize attention and virality. | **Relationship Cultivation**: Building and maintaining two-way interpersonal, professional, or community ties. |
| **2. Communication Flow** | **Asymmetric / One-to-Many**: A creator broadcasts to millions of passive or reacting followers (monologue). | **Symmetric / Many-to-Many**: Dialogic, reciprocal communication between recognized peers (conversation). |
| **3. Central Structural Asset** | **The Media Asset**: The algorithm curates and surfaces content based on engagement metrics, regardless of who created it. | **The Profile & Network Graph**: The architecture centers on user profiles, mutual connections, and social identity. |
| **4. Performance Metrics** | Views, impressions, watch time, shares, and algorithmic reach. | Connection strength, message frequency, mutual trust, and community cohesion. |
| **5. Platform Archetypes** | YouTube, TikTok, Public Twitter/X, Instagram Reels. | LinkedIn, Facebook Groups, Private Slack/Discord servers, Professional Alumni networks. |

### 3. Organizational Application: Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)
Modern corporate enterprises leverage both channels strategically to manage stakeholder perception, but the communication dynamics differ sharply:

* **Social Media for CSR Broadcasting (Awareness & Visibility)**: Organizations use YouTube mini-documentaries, TikTok reels, and Instagram infographics to broadcast large-scale environmental sustainability initiatives, charitable donations, and ESG compliance. While this achieves massive reach, it carries the severe risk of consumer backlash—audiences frequently call out "greenwashing" or performative activism in public comment sections.
* **Social Networking for CSR Dialogue (Stakeholder Engagement)**: On professional networks such as LinkedIn or specialized community groups, organizations engage in substantive stakeholder dialogue. Corporations share technical sustainability reports, engage with civil society leaders, recruit diverse talent, and solicit feedback from environmental NGOs. This builds genuine social license to operate through authentic, verifiable peer interaction.

### 4. Conclusion
While the boundaries between both concepts continue to blur through platform convergence (such as LinkedIn introducing video feeds or YouTube adding community tabs), the analytical distinction remains crucial. Organizations and information professionals who fail to understand this difference treat social networks like broadcast bullhorns, alienating communities that demand authentic reciprocal engagement.
