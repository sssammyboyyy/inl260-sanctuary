# Theme 2: The Political Economy of Information

## 🎯 Syllabus Outcomes (Study Guide LO 1, 2, 3)

* **Exam Section**: Essay / Discussion Questions (50–60 Marks) & MCQs.

## 📖 The Narrative Hook
Imagine a nation where the dictator makes it illegal for the word "poverty" to appear in any book, newspaper, or university lecture. In Malawi under "Life President" Dr. Hastings Kamuzu Banda (1964–1994), possessing an unauthorized pamphlet got you thrown in jail without trial. Why would a government spend millions hunting down books and censoring songs? Because **information is the supreme political weapon**: if you control the words people hear, you control the thoughts they can think.

## 🏛️ Core Academic Frameworks

### 1. Four Corners / Definitions of the Information Society (Frank Webster)
What makes a society an "Information Society"?
1. **Technological**: The explosion of high-speed digital networks, computers, and telecommunications uniting global operations.
2. **Economic**: The knowledge sector (creation, processing, distribution) accounts for the majority of a nation's Gross Domestic Product (GDP).
3. **Occupational**: The predominance of knowledge workers (managers, coders, educators, analysts) over traditional agricultural or manufacturing laborers.
4. **Spatial**: Networks and fiber optics transcend geographic distance, enabling real-time remote collaboration.
5. **Cultural**: Everyday life is thoroughly saturated with digital media, online symbols, and continuous information streams.

### 2. Paul Sturges: The Political Economy of Information in Malawi (1964–1994)
* **Context**: President Hastings Kamuzu Banda ruled Malawi with an iron fist for 30 years after independence.
* **The Mechanism of Control (The Censorship Board)**:
  * Established under the Censorship and Control of Entertainments Act (1968).
  * Systematically banned thousands of books, songs (e.g. Simon & Garfunkel), magazines, and plays.
  * Banned foreign journalists; state radio (Malawi Broadcasting Corporation) broadcast exclusively state propaganda.
  * Television was completely forbidden to prevent exposure to foreign political ideas.
* **The Societal Cost — "Information Poverty"**:
  * Suppressed agricultural science and health information, causing severe malnutrition and preventable disease outbreaks.
  * Destroyed academic research; university lecturers were imprisoned for teaching Marxist economics or sociology.
* **Sturges’ Core Thesis**:
  * *Information is never neutral*. In authoritarian political economies, the state creates an **artificial information monopoly** because free information flows are an existential threat to elite political survival.

### 3. Information as a Commodity vs. Public Good
* **Information as a Public Good**: Historically treated as non-rivalrous and non-excludable—shared freely within libraries, universities, and communities to enlighten society.
* **Commodification of Information**: Under contemporary capitalism, information is enclosed through intellectual property laws (patents, copyrights), digital rights management (DRM), paywalls, and private corporate monopolies (Google, Elsevier, Meta). Knowledge becomes something you must purchase.

### 4. Mark Graham et al. (2015): The Geography of Information
* Does the internet eliminate geographic inequality? **No.**
* Graham’s research shows massive **digital spatial unevenness**:
  * The Global North produces over 80% of indexed digital knowledge, Wikipedia articles, and academic citations.
  * The Global South (especially Sub-Saharan Africa) remains a passive consumer or is represented online primarily through foreign lenses (digital colonialism).
