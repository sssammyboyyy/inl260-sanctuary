# Theme 7: Social Media vs. Social Networking

## 🎯 Syllabus Outcomes (Study Guide LO 1, 2, 3)
* **Required Reading**: Tugtekin & Koc (2019) *Understanding the relationship between new media literacy, communication skills and democratic participation*; Social Media as a new communication platform.
* **Exam Section**: Essay / Discussion Questions (50–60 Marks) & MCQs.

---

## 📖 The Narrative Hook
If you stand on a stage with a microphone shouting into a loudspeaker to 5,000 strangers who applaud or boo, you are using **Social Media**. If you walk into a dinner party, shake someone's hand, listen to their story, exchange business cards, and introduce them to your friend, you are doing **Social Networking**. Mixing them up is why so many companies fail online.

---

## 🏛️ Core Academic Frameworks

### 1. The Definitive Distinction

| Structural Dimension | Social Media | Social Networking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Primary Purpose** | **Broadcast & Consumption**: Distributing media assets (videos, articles, images) to maximize audience reach and engagement. | **Relationship Cultivation**: Building, deepening, and maintaining two-way interpersonal and professional connections. |
| **2. Communication Architecture** | **Asymmetric / One-to-Many**: Monologic broadcast from a central creator to a dispersed audience. | **Symmetric / Many-to-Many**: Dialogic, interactive, conversational exchange between peers. |
| **3. Central Organizing Asset** | **The Content Asset**: Virality of the video or post dictates visibility via algorithmic recommendations. | **The Profile & Graph**: Social identity, mutual contacts, and shared interest groups dictate connection. |
| **4. Core Success Metric** | Views, impressions, watch time, virality coefficients, subscriber count. | Relationship depth, community trust, message reciprocity, network density. |
| **5. Canonical Platforms** | YouTube, TikTok, Public Twitter/X, Instagram Reels. | LinkedIn, Facebook Groups, Private Slack/Discord networks, WhatsApp community groups. |

### 2. User Engagement Dynamics
* **Passive Consumption vs Active Participation**:
  * Social Media skews heavily toward the **90-9-1 Rule**: 90% of users are lurkers (viewers), 9% interact lightly (like/comment), and only 1% create the content.
  * Social Networking demands active bidirectional participation to sustain social capital and reciprocal value.

### 3. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Communication
How do commercial organizations navigate these two channels for CSR?
* **Broadcasting on Social Media**:
  * Companies publish polished video campaigns highlighting environmental sustainability, renewable energy investments, and community philanthropy.
  * *The Trap*: High risk of audience cynicism—consumers quickly flag performative "greenwashing" or "woke-washing" in public comment sections.
* **Dialogue on Social Networks**:
  * Companies utilize professional networks (LinkedIn) and dedicated community forums to engage directly with civil society leaders, academic researchers, and affected local stakeholders.
  * Fosters genuine institutional transparency and stakeholder co-creation.

## ⚡ Examiner Trap Alert
* ⚠️ **Trap**: In an essay question on this topic, do NOT simply list platforms (e.g. "TikTok is media, LinkedIn is networking"). You MUST analyze the **underlying communication architecture** (asymmetric broadcasting vs symmetric dialogue) to score 15+ marks!
